# Environmental Guide for Hydroponic Tomatoes

## Optimal Temperature Ranges
- **Daytime Air Temperature**: 21°C to 26°C (70°F to 79°F). Temperatures above 30°C (86°F) can cause pollen sterility and blossom drop.
- **Nighttime Air Temperature**: 16°C to 18°C (61°F to 64°F). A distinct day/night temperature differential (DIF) of 5-8°C is crucial for generative growth, flower development, and fruit flavor.
- **Root Zone / Water Temperature**: 18°C to 22°C (64°F to 72°F). Water temperatures above 24°C (75°F) hold significantly less dissolved oxygen and dramatically increase the risk of Pythium (root rot).

## Humidity Management and VPD
- **Relative Humidity (RH)**: Optimal range is 60% to 70%.
- **High Humidity (>80%)**: Reduces transpiration, halting the upward flow of water and mobile nutrients (like Calcium), leading directly to Blossom End Rot and fostering fungal diseases (Botrytis, Powdery Mildew).
- **Low Humidity (<50%)**: Causes excessive transpiration. Plants close stomata to save water, halting photosynthesis and CO2 uptake, leading to stressed, slow growth.
- **VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)**: The best metric for humidity. Optimal VPD for tomatoes is:
  - Vegetative: 0.8 to 1.0 kPa
  - Flowering/Fruiting: 1.0 to 1.2 kPa

## Light Requirements
- **DLI (Daily Light Integral)**: Measures the total amount of light delivered over 24 hours. Tomatoes are high-light plants requiring a DLI of 20 to 30 mol/m²/day for optimal yield.
- **Photoperiod**: 14 to 18 hours of light per day. Tomatoes require a period of absolute darkness (minimum 6-8 hours) to respire and move sugars from leaves to roots/fruit. Continuous 24-hour light will damage tomato plants.
- **Intensity**: Target 400-600 µmol/m²/s of PAR at the canopy level during the light cycle.

## Water Temperature and Dissolved Oxygen (DO)
- Cold water holds more oxygen. Maintain nutrient solution between 18-20°C (64-68°F).
- Optimal DO levels are 6-8 ppm (mg/L).
- Use air stones or venturi aerators in deep water culture (DWC) or reservoirs. Low DO causes roots to drown, turn brown, and become susceptible to pathogens.

## Air Circulation and CO2
- **Airflow**: Maintain constant, gentle air movement (about 1 meter per second) across the crop canopy. This disrupts the microclimate boundary layer around leaves, improving transpiration and CO2 uptake, and preventing fungal spores from settling.
- **CO2**: Normal atmospheric CO2 is ~400 ppm. Supplementing CO2 to 800-1200 ppm in a sealed greenhouse significantly increases photosynthesis and yield, provided light and temperature are also optimized.

## Moisture and Water Flow Management
- **Irrigation Strategy**: In media-based systems (rockwool, coco), allow for 10-20% runoff during each irrigation cycle to flush out accumulated salts and pull fresh oxygen into the root zone.
- **Dry-backs**: Allow the media to dry back slightly between watering cycles. This mild stress promotes a healthy, expansive root system and pushes the plant toward generative (fruiting) growth rather than purely vegetative growth.

## Signs of Environmental Stress
- **Leaf Rolling (Upward curl)**: Often a response to high heat, high light intensity, or severe pruning. The plant is trying to reduce exposed surface area.
- **Purple Stems/Leaf Undersides**: Indicates cold stress, which inhibits Phosphorus uptake even if P is present in the solution.
- **Blossom Drop**: Flowers abort and fall off before setting fruit. Caused by temperature extremes (>30°C or <13°C), low humidity, or extreme drought.

## Seasonal Adjustments
- **Winter**: Focus on maintaining nighttime temperatures and supplementing light (DLI). Dehumidification is critical as cold air holds less moisture, and heating raises RH.
- **Summer**: Focus on cooling (evaporative cooling pads, shade cloth) and lowering reservoir temperatures. EC should be run slightly lower (more dilute) to account for increased plant water uptake.
