# pH and EC Management in Hydroponics

## Optimal pH Range for Tomatoes
The optimal pH range for hydroponic tomatoes is **5.5 to 6.5**.
The ideal target setpoint is usually **5.8 to 6.0**.

## Why pH Matters
pH (Potential of Hydrogen) determines the solubility of mineral nutrients in the water. If the pH is outside the optimal range, certain nutrients precipitate out of solution, becoming unavailable to the plant roots. This is called "nutrient lockout."
- **pH < 5.5 (Too Acidic)**: Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), and Molybdenum (Mo) become less available. Risks of heavy metal toxicity (e.g., Manganese, Copper) increase.
- **pH > 6.5 (Too Alkaline)**: Iron (Fe), Manganese (Mn), Boron (B), Copper (Cu), and Zinc (Zn) become tightly bound and unavailable. Iron deficiency is the most rapid and common symptom of high pH.

## pH Drift Causes and Corrections
pH naturally drifts in active hydroponic systems due to root exudates, microbial activity, and active ion uptake.
- **Upward Drift (Increasing pH)**: Usually signifies that plants are taking up nitrate ions (anions) faster than water, which is a sign of healthy vegetative growth. Roots release bicarbonate to balance charge, raising pH.
- **Downward Drift (Decreasing pH)**: Can signify ammonium uptake, excessive heat, root rot (bacterial action), or that plants are taking up water faster than nutrients.
- **Correction**: Check pH daily. Allow it to drift naturally within the 5.5-6.5 range to ensure all nutrients get optimal absorption windows (e.g., Ca is absorbed better near 6.2, Fe better near 5.5). Only adjust when it approaches the boundaries.

## EC/TDS Meaning and Measurement
- **EC (Electrical Conductivity)**: Measures the ability of water to conduct electricity, directly proportional to the amount of dissolved mineral salts (ions). Measured in milliSiemens per centimeter (mS/cm) or microSiemens (µS/cm).
- **TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)**: An estimate of salt mass based on EC, measured in Parts Per Million (ppm). TDS meters just measure EC and apply a conversion factor (usually 0.5 or 0.7). Always use EC for accuracy to avoid conversion confusion.

## Optimal EC Ranges for Tomato Growth Stages
- **Seedlings**: 0.8 - 1.2 mS/cm
- **Vegetative Growth**: 1.5 - 2.0 mS/cm
- **Flowering**: 2.0 - 2.5 mS/cm
- **Fruiting/Harvest**: 2.5 - 3.5 mS/cm
Tomatoes are highly tolerant of high EC during fruiting. Higher EC (mild salt stress) actually increases fruit flavor and Brix (sugar) levels, though it may slightly reduce overall fruit size.

## How to Raise/Lower pH Safely
- **Lowering pH (pH Down)**: Use Phosphoric Acid (most common for bloom stage), Nitric Acid (good for veg stage), or Sulfuric Acid. Add slowly, diluted in water first, never directly to concentrated nutrients.
- **Raising pH (pH Up)**: Use Potassium Hydroxide (KOH) or Potassium Silicate.
- **Rule of Thumb**: Add small amounts (e.g., 1-2 ml per 10 gallons), wait 15-30 minutes for complete mixing, then retest. Overcorrecting back and forth causes unstable pH swings and plant stress.

## Relationship Between pH, EC, and Nutrient Uptake
Monitoring daily changes in EC and water levels tells you exactly what the plant is doing:
1. **EC drops, Water drops**: Plant is taking up more nutrients than water. *Action*: Increase reservoir EC.
2. **EC rises, Water drops**: Plant is taking up more water than nutrients (transpiring heavily, likely due to high heat/low humidity). *Action*: Decrease reservoir EC by adding fresh water.
3. **EC stable, Water drops**: Perfect balance. Plant is taking up water and nutrients in the exact ratio provided. *Action*: Maintain current EC.

## Common pH/EC Problems and Solutions
- **Sudden pH Plunge**: Often indicates root disease (Pythium/root rot). Check roots for brown slime and foul odor. Clean the reservoir and apply hydrogen peroxide or beneficial microbes.
- **Constant High EC Drift**: The nutrient solution is too strong for the current environment. High temperatures increase transpiration, causing salt buildup. Dilute the reservoir immediately to prevent tip burn and leaf necrosis.
- **White Crust on Media/Pots**: Salt accumulation from high EC or hard water. Flush the system with pH 5.8 water to dissolve and wash away excess salts.
