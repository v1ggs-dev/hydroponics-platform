# Pest Management in Hydroponic Systems

## Common Hydroponic Pests
**Aphids**
- Small, soft-bodied insects (green, black, or peach). Cluster on new growth and undersides of leaves.
- **Damage**: Suck plant sap, causing curled, stunted leaves. Excrete sticky "honeydew" which promotes black sooty mold.

**Whiteflies**
- Tiny, moth-like white flying insects. Erupt in a cloud when the plant is disturbed.
- **Damage**: Sap-sucking. Cause leaf yellowing, stunting, and transmit severe viruses (e.g., TYLCV). Also excrete honeydew.

**Spider Mites**
- Microscopic arachnids, often reddish or pale yellow. Prefer hot, dry conditions.
- **Damage**: Piercing-sucking feeding causes yellow/white stippling on leaves. Severe infestations produce fine webbing and lead to leaf death.

**Thrips**
- Tiny, slender, cigar-shaped insects. Very fast moving.
- **Damage**: Scrape plant tissue and suck liquids. Leaves show silvery, scarred patches with tiny black fecal specks. Transmit devastating viruses (e.g., Tomato Spotted Wilt Virus - TSWV).

**Fungus Gnats**
- Small, dark flies resembling mosquitoes. Adults fly near the base of the plant.
- **Damage**: Adults are a nuisance, but the larvae in the root zone feed on root hairs, causing stunting and providing entry points for root rot pathogens (Pythium).

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Approach
IPM is a strategy emphasizing prevention, monitoring, and control using the least toxic methods first.
1. **Cultural**: System sanitation, environmental control, resistant varieties.
2. **Physical/Mechanical**: Traps, screens, pruning.
3. **Biological**: Introduction of natural predators.
4. **Chemical**: Pesticides (only as a last resort).

## Biological Controls (Beneficial Insects)
Introduce biologicals preventatively or at the very first sign of pests.
- **For Spider Mites**: *Phytoseiulus persimilis* (predatory mites - require high humidity), *Amblyseius californicus*.
- **For Aphids**: Ladybugs (*Hippodamia convergens*), Green Lacewings (*Chrysoperla carnea*), Aphidius wasps (parasitic wasps).
- **For Whiteflies**: *Encarsia formosa* (parasitic wasps), *Amblyseius swirskii* (predatory mites).
- **For Thrips**: *Orius insidiosus* (minute pirate bugs), *Amblyseius cucumeris*.
- **For Fungus Gnat Larvae**: *Steinernema feltiae* (beneficial nematodes), *Bacillus thuringiensis var. israelensis* (Bti - biological larvicide).

## Organic Treatments
- **Insecticidal Soaps**: Target soft-bodied insects (aphids, whiteflies, spider mites). Works only on contact by disrupting cell membranes.
- **Horticultural Oils (Neem Oil)**: Acts as a repellent, feeding inhibitor, and disrupts insect life cycles. Spray late in the day to prevent leaf burn under intense grow lights.
- **Pyrethrin**: Derived from chrysanthemum flowers. Fast-acting contact killer for most insects. Degrades rapidly in sunlight.
- **Spinosad**: Organic bacterial derivative. Highly effective against thrips and caterpillars.

## Chemical Treatments
*Use only when organic and biological methods fail. Obey all local regulations regarding food crops.*
- **Azadirachtin**: Highly concentrated active ingredient from Neem.
- **Bifenthrin / Permethrin**: Synthetic pyrethroids. Broad-spectrum, will kill beneficial insects as well.
- **Imidacloprid**: Systemic insecticide (often applied to root zone). Highly effective against aphids and whiteflies, but persists in the plant and environment.

## Prevention Strategies Specific to Hydroponics
- **Sanitation**: Clean and sterilize the entire system (reservoirs, lines, trays) with 10% bleach or hydrogen peroxide between crops.
- **Exclusion**: Use fine-mesh screens on all air intakes and exhaust vents. Keep doors closed.
- **Quarantine**: Never introduce plants from soil-based environments or outside nurseries directly into the hydroponic room. Grow from seed internally.
- **Trapping**: Hang yellow sticky traps (for fungus gnats, whiteflies, aphids) and blue sticky traps (specifically for thrips) at canopy level to monitor pest populations early.
- **Algae Control**: Cover reservoirs and exposed media (like rockwool blocks) to block light. Algae attracts fungus gnats and shore flies.

## Disease-Pest Interaction
Pests are vectors for pathogens. Controlling pests is essential for disease management.
- **Whiteflies** transmit Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV).
- **Thrips** transmit Tomato Spotted Wilt Virus (TSWV).
- **Aphids** transmit Cucumber Mosaic Virus (CMV).
- **Fungus Gnat larvae** create microscopic wounds in roots that allow Pythium and Fusarium fungi to enter and destroy the root system.
