# Tomato Diseases Guide

## Tomato___Bacterial_spot
- **Pathogen**: *Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria*
- **Cause**: Bacterial infection spread by splashing water, infected seeds, or contaminated equipment.
- **Symptoms**: Small, water-soaked, circular spots on leaves, stems, and fruit. Spots eventually turn brown or black with a yellow halo. Leaves may turn yellow and drop prematurely. Fruit spots become raised and scabby.
- **Favorable Conditions**: High humidity (>85%), warm temperatures (24-30°C / 75-86°F), and prolonged periods of leaf wetness.
- **Organic Treatments**: Copper-based bactericides (e.g., copper hydroxide, copper octanoate) applied preventatively. Neem oil for mild suppression.
- **Chemical Treatments**: Streptomycin (where legal), Mancozeb + Copper tank mix for enhanced efficacy.
- **Prevention**: Use certified disease-free seeds. Avoid overhead watering. Maintain proper spacing for airflow. Sanitize hydroponic equipment between crops. Remove and destroy infected plant debris.

## Tomato___Early_blight
- **Pathogen**: *Alternaria solani*
- **Cause**: Fungal pathogen surviving in plant debris or soil/media. Spores are windborne or water-splashed.
- **Symptoms**: Starts on older, lower leaves as small brown spots that enlarge into concentric rings (target-like). Leaves may turn yellow and die. Can cause dark, sunken lesions on stems and leathery, dark spots on fruit near the stem end.
- **Favorable Conditions**: High humidity, moderate to warm temperatures (24-29°C / 75-84°F), alternating wet and dry periods.
- **Organic Treatments**: Copper fungicides, Bacillus subtilis biofungicides, potassium bicarbonate.
- **Chemical Treatments**: Chlorothalonil, Azoxystrobin, Mancozeb.
- **Prevention**: Bottom-water only. Improve ventilation. Prune lower leaves to increase airflow and prevent water splash. Maintain consistent nutrient levels to avoid plant stress.

## Tomato___Late_blight
- **Pathogen**: *Phytophthora infestans*
- **Cause**: Oomycete (water mold) pathogen. Spores travel long distances on wind currents. Highly destructive.
- **Symptoms**: Rapidly spreading, large, irregularly shaped, water-soaked lesions on leaves that turn pale green to brown. White fuzzy mold may appear on leaf undersides in high humidity. Stems show dark brown lesions. Fruit develop firm, dark brown, greasy-looking spots.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Cool to moderate temperatures (15-21°C / 60-70°F), high humidity (near 100%), and continuous leaf wetness.
- **Organic Treatments**: Copper-based fungicides (must be applied preventatively, ineffective once severe).
- **Chemical Treatments**: Mefenoxam, Chlorothalonil, Cymoxanil, Propamocarb.
- **Prevention**: Monitor local late blight tracking systems. Keep greenhouse humidity below 85%. Ensure excellent air circulation. Eliminate all infected plants immediately (do not compost).

## Tomato___Leaf_Mold
- **Pathogen**: *Passalora fulva* (formerly *Fulvia fulva* / *Cladosporium fulvum*)
- **Cause**: Fungal spores spread by air currents, splashing water, or workers. Common in greenhouse/hydroponic environments.
- **Symptoms**: Pale greenish-yellow spots appear on the upper leaf surface. The corresponding lower surface develops an olive-green to gray velvety fungal growth. Older leaves are affected first. Leaves eventually wither and die.
- **Favorable Conditions**: High relative humidity (>85%), temperatures between 21-24°C (70-75°F). Pathogen cannot infect if leaf surfaces are dry.
- **Organic Treatments**: Ensure humidity control. Potassium bicarbonate, Neem oil, botanical oils.
- **Chemical Treatments**: Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, Difenoconazole.
- **Prevention**: Keep relative humidity below 80%. Increase heating and ventilation, especially at night. Prune lower leaves. Space plants adequately. Use resistant varieties (e.g., those with Cf resistance genes).

## Tomato___Septoria_leaf_spot
- **Pathogen**: *Septoria lycopersici*
- **Cause**: Fungal pathogen that overwinters in plant debris and weeds. Spread by water splashing.
- **Symptoms**: Numerous small, circular spots on older leaves. Spots have dark brown margins and tan/gray centers, often with tiny black specks (pycnidia) in the center. Leaves may turn yellow, wither, and drop. Fruit is rarely affected.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Moderate temperatures (20-25°C / 68-77°F), high humidity, and prolonged leaf wetness.
- **Organic Treatments**: Copper fungicides, Bacillus pumilus.
- **Chemical Treatments**: Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb.
- **Prevention**: Avoid wetting foliage. Remove lower infected leaves. Maintain proper spacing. Sanitize tools. Rotate crops or fully replace hydroponic media between cycles.

## Tomato___Spider_mites_Two-spotted_spider_mite
- **Pathogen/Pest**: *Tetranychus urticae*
- **Cause**: Microscopic arachnids that pierce plant cells and suck out contents. Dispersed by wind, humans, or equipment.
- **Symptoms**: Tiny yellow or white speckles (stippling) on the upper leaf surface. As damage progresses, leaves turn yellow, bronze, or gray, and may drop. Fine, silken webbing often visible on leaf undersides and stems, especially in severe infestations.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Hot, dry conditions (low humidity). Stressed plants are highly susceptible.
- **Organic Treatments**: Insecticidal soap, horticultural oils (Neem oil, canola oil), predatory mites (e.g., *Phytoseiulus persimilis*, *Neoseiulus californicus*).
- **Chemical Treatments**: Abamectin, Spiromesifen, Bifenazate (miticides). Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides which kill predators and exacerbate spider mite outbreaks.
- **Prevention**: Maintain adequate humidity (around 60-70%). Ensure plants are not water-stressed. Regularly inspect leaf undersides with a hand lens. Quarantine new plants.

## Tomato___Target_Spot
- **Pathogen**: *Corynespora cassiicola*
- **Cause**: Fungal pathogen. Spores spread by air currents and splashing water.
- **Symptoms**: Small, dark brown spots on leaves that enlarge and develop concentric rings, similar to early blight but often with a more distinct yellow halo. Lesions on stems and fruit. Fruit lesions start as small, sunken spots that enlarge and become dark and cracked.
- **Favorable Conditions**: High humidity (approaching 100%), prolonged leaf wetness, and warm temperatures (20-28°C / 68-82°F).
- **Organic Treatments**: Copper fungicides, biologicals (e.g., Bacillus subtilis).
- **Chemical Treatments**: Chlorothalonil, Azoxystrobin, Mancozeb.
- **Prevention**: Reduce humidity through ventilation. Avoid overhead irrigation. Remove infected leaves. Optimize plant nutrition.

## Tomato___Tomato_Yellow_Leaf_Curl_Virus
- **Pathogen**: Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV)
- **Cause**: Viral disease transmitted primarily by the sweetpotato whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*). Not seed-borne.
- **Symptoms**: Stunted plant growth. Leaves are small, cup upward, and have a yellow/pale green margin. Internodes are shortened. Flowers may drop, and fruit production is severely reduced or halted. Fruit already set may ripen normally but will be small.
- **Favorable Conditions**: High whitefly populations, warm temperatures favoring vector breeding.
- **Organic Treatments**: None for the virus. Control vectors using reflective mulches, insecticidal soaps, Neem oil, or yellow sticky traps.
- **Chemical Treatments**: None for the virus. Imidacloprid, Dinotefuran, or Spiromesifen to control the whitefly vector.
- **Prevention**: Use TYLCV-resistant tomato varieties. Exclude whiteflies using fine-mesh insect screens (e.g., 50-mesh). Control weeds around the greenhouse. Remove and destroy infected plants immediately.

## Tomato___Tomato_mosaic_virus
- **Pathogen**: Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV) / Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)
- **Cause**: Highly contagious virus transmitted mechanically by hands, tools, clothing, or contaminated seeds/debris. Not insect-transmitted.
- **Symptoms**: Leaves show a light and dark green mottled or mosaic pattern. Leaves may be distorted, blistered, or fern-like. Plants may be stunted. Fruit may show internal browning, uneven ripening, or yellow rings.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Any conditions; highly stable virus that survives in dried debris for years.
- **Organic Treatments**: None. Infected plants cannot be cured.
- **Chemical Treatments**: None.
- **Prevention**: Use resistant varieties (look for TMV/ToMV resistance). Enforce strict sanitation: wash hands with soap and water or use milk dips before handling plants. Do not smoke tobacco near plants. Disinfect tools with a 10% bleach solution or commercial virucide. Remove and destroy infected plants immediately.

## Tomato___healthy
- **Characteristics**: Healthy tomato plants exhibit vibrant, uniform green leaves without spots, mottling, or yellowing. Leaves should be flat to slightly textured, not curled or wilted. Stems should be sturdy. Flowers should be bright yellow and proceed to set fruit without dropping prematurely.
- **Maintenance for Health**: Maintain optimal temperature (day 21-26°C, night 16-18°C), humidity (60-70%), pH (5.5-6.5), and EC based on growth stage. Ensure adequate airflow (VPD between 0.8-1.2 kPa). Provide sufficient light (DLI 20-30 mol/m²/d). Practice regular pruning and suckering to optimize energy usage and airflow. Inspect weekly for early signs of pests or pathogens.
