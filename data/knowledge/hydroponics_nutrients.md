# Hydroponics Nutrient Management Guide for Tomatoes

## Macro Nutrients (N, P, K)
**Nitrogen (N)**
- **Role**: Essential for vegetative growth, leaf and stem formation, and chlorophyll production.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: Lower (older) leaves turn uniformly pale green or yellow (chlorosis). Stunted growth and thin stems.
- **Excess Symptoms**: Extremely dark green foliage, excessive vegetative growth at the expense of flowers/fruit, delayed flowering.

**Phosphorus (P)**
- **Role**: Critical for root development, energy transfer (ATP), flowering, and fruit set.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: Stunted growth. Older leaves turn unusually dark green, often developing a purplish tint on the undersides and stems.
- **Excess Symptoms**: Rarely directly toxic, but high P can lock out Zinc (Zn), Iron (Fe), and Copper (Cu), leading to their respective deficiency symptoms.

**Potassium (K)**
- **Role**: Regulates stomatal opening (water use), enzyme activation, sugar transport, and fruit quality/size.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: Margins of older leaves turn yellow, then brown and necrotic (burnt edges). Weak stems and poor fruit quality (uneven ripening).
- **Excess Symptoms**: Can induce Magnesium (Mg) and Calcium (Ca) deficiencies.

## Secondary Nutrients (Ca, Mg, S)
**Calcium (Ca)**
- **Role**: Structural component of cell walls and membranes. Important for root growth.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: New leaves are distorted, crinkled, or necrotic at the tips. Blossom End Rot (BER) on fruit (sunken, black, leathery spot on the bottom of the tomato).
- **Excess Symptoms**: Can lock out Potassium (K) and Magnesium (Mg).

**Magnesium (Mg)**
- **Role**: Central atom of the chlorophyll molecule; essential for photosynthesis.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between the veins) on older leaves, while veins remain green. Leaves may eventually develop necrotic spots.
- **Excess Symptoms**: Can interfere with Calcium (Ca) and Potassium (K) uptake.

**Sulfur (S)**
- **Role**: Component of amino acids and proteins.
- **Deficiency Symptoms**: Overall yellowing of the entire plant, similar to N deficiency, but often starts on younger leaves first.
- **Excess Symptoms**: Reduced leaf size and overall stunted growth.

## Micro Nutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, B, Cu, Mo)
- **Iron (Fe)**: Crucial for chlorophyll synthesis. Deficiency shows interveinal chlorosis on *new* leaves (veins stay sharply green).
- **Manganese (Mn)**: Enzyme activation. Deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis on young leaves, similar to Iron, but with necrotic spotting.
- **Zinc (Zn)**: Hormone regulation (auxins). Deficiency causes stunted new growth ("rosetting") and small, narrow leaves.
- **Boron (B)**: Cell wall formation and reproductive tissue. Deficiency causes death of the terminal bud, brittle stems, and poor fruit set/deformed fruit.
- **Copper (Cu)**: Photosynthesis and respiration. Deficiency causes young leaves to become dark green, twisted, and wilted.
- **Molybdenum (Mo)**: Nitrogen utilization. Deficiency causes pale green leaves with marginal necrosis, similar to N deficiency but with rolled edges.

## Optimal Nutrient Solution Composition for Tomatoes
Tomatoes are heavy feeders, especially of Potassium and Calcium during fruiting.
Target elemental concentrations (ppm or mg/L):
- Nitrogen (N): 150-200 ppm (Higher in veg, lower in fruiting)
- Phosphorus (P): 40-50 ppm
- Potassium (K): 300-400 ppm (Increases significantly during fruiting)
- Calcium (Ca): 150-200 ppm (Crucial to prevent Blossom End Rot)
- Magnesium (Mg): 50-60 ppm
- Sulfur (S): 50-100 ppm
- Iron (Fe): 2-5 ppm
- Manganese (Mn): 0.5-1.0 ppm
- Zinc (Zn): 0.3-0.5 ppm
- Boron (B): 0.3-0.5 ppm
- Copper (Cu): 0.05-0.1 ppm
- Molybdenum (Mo): 0.01-0.05 ppm

## EC/TDS Ranges by Growth Stage
Electrical Conductivity (EC) measures the total dissolved salts in the solution.
- **Seedling Stage**: 0.8 - 1.2 mS/cm
- **Vegetative Stage**: 1.5 - 2.0 mS/cm
- **Flowering Stage**: 2.0 - 2.5 mS/cm
- **Fruiting Stage**: 2.5 - 3.5 mS/cm
*(Note: 1 mS/cm = 1000 µS/cm ≈ 500-700 ppm TDS depending on the conversion factor used by the meter).*

## Adjusting Nutrient Solutions
- **Increasing EC**: Add equal parts of your base nutrient concentrates (e.g., Part A and Part B) to the reservoir.
- **Decreasing EC**: Add fresh, pH-adjusted RO or filtered water to the reservoir to dilute the concentration.
- **Nutrient Lockout**: If EC is high but plants show deficiencies, a nutrient imbalance or pH issue is causing lockout. Do not add more nutrients. Flush the system with pH-adjusted water (or half-strength solution) and replace the reservoir with a fresh batch.
- **Reservoir Changes**: Completely dump and replace the nutrient reservoir every 7-14 days to prevent the buildup of unused specific ions and metabolic waste.

## Common Deficiency Patterns
- **Old Leaves Yellowing**: Mobile nutrients (N, P, K, Mg). The plant moves these to new growth.
- **New Leaves Yellowing/Distorted**: Immobile nutrients (Ca, Fe, B, Zn). The plant cannot move these from old tissue, so new growth suffers immediately.
- **Blossom End Rot**: Always a Calcium issue, but often caused by environmental factors (inconsistent watering, high humidity, or high K/Mg competing with Ca uptake) rather than a lack of Ca in the reservoir.
